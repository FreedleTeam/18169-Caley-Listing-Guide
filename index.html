I don’t usually drop my compensation this low. I know how hard this sale is going to be, and I’m rooting for you. Why I think now is the right time

I want to be upfront about two things. I’m your agent, not your financial advisor, and I get paid when you sell, though I’ve cut that pay to help you do this. And nobody knows what rates or prices will do. Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

With that said, here’s why I think moving now makes sense.

Waiting doesn’t fix the math. If you hold out until your house is worth $800,000, the house you want will likely have gone up by the same percentage, and because it costs more, that’s more dollars. You’d save about $70,000 at closing today but pay about $100,000 more for the next house.

Buy now

About $15,000

The cost of today’s 6.75% rate until you can refinance to 6%, assuming about 2 years, plus refinance costs.

Wait for $800,000

About $53,600

The wider price gap of about $24,800, borrowed at 6% and paid over 30 years.

Under these assumptions, waiting costs roughly $38,000 more. These are estimates built on Lewiston at its $965,000 list price, a VA loan with nothing down, and both homes rising by the same percentage.

The wait could be long. Getting to $800,000 will likely take 5+ years. That’s years in a house you’re ready to leave, waiting on a market neither of us controls.

Rates aren’t a reason to wait. Your house only gets to $800,000 if rates come down. If rates come down, you can refinance. Buying now means paying today’s rate for a while, not forever.

And if you decide to stay put, that’s okay

This is a real check to write, and only you two know what feels right. Staying is a legitimate choice, not a failure. If you’d rather wait, I’ll keep watching the market with you and we’ll revisit when it makes sense. But if you’re asking what I’d do in your shoes, I’d go.
